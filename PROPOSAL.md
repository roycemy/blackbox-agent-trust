# Blackbox proposal

I talked with Yash for about twenty minutes on Sunday. He is a friend, not a client I found online. These notes are from that conversation. I had looked at a few privacy and product sites beforehand and showed them to him on my laptop.

## Client Brief

**Client:** Yash Shah, a junior at Babson College. He is ideating Blackbox, a local-first trust layer for personal AI agents. It is not a company and nothing is shipping. He asked me to make a site he can send to classmates when he explains the idea.

**Purpose:** Give a visitor the idea in one pass: agents can act, and the owner should still be able to see what happened, set limits, and make the last call.

**Audience:** Babson students and other people who already let an assistant draft, send, or buy things. They need plain language. They do not need a login, a price, or a feature matrix.

**Key action:** Read how the gate works, then check the early-access status. The status has to say that signup is not open. Yash does not want an email address stored on this site.

**Pages needed:**

- Home. The promise, three pieces (receipts, rules, the gate), and the early-access status.
- How it works. One section for each piece, with an example that is obviously fake.
- Why. The short argument for why the agent company should not be the one auditing itself.

**Content status:** Yash talked through the lines he wanted. I have that copy. I do not have a logo file, photos, or a brand guide. He said a letter B in a box is enough for now. Still needed from him later: a real logo if he makes one, and a yes or no after he opens the live site on his phone. A signup form stays out until he has a backend and a privacy notice. He was clear that illustrative receipts must not look like a live feed from his agents.

**Style preferences:** Near-black background, off-white text, one acid green used like a status light. Labels in a monospace face, sentences in a plain sans. He said it should feel like a record you keep, not a pitch deck. He does not want robot photos, a purple gradient, a laptop mockup, the phrase "AI-powered" as the headline, or three floating cards with generic icons. He does not want the page to look like you can log in.

**Inspiration sites:**

- [Proton](https://proton.me). He liked that privacy is the point of the page, not a footer link. He did not want their long feature grid.
- [Signal](https://signal.org). He liked the short sentences. He said a site that only says one thing would be too thin for three ideas.
- [Linear](https://linear.app). He liked the dark ground and the tight type. He did not want it to look like software you open and use.
- [Apple Privacy](https://www.apple.com/privacy/). He liked the feeling that the device is on your side. He did not want photography or a campaign look. Blackbox is a concept, not a product ad.
- [1Password](https://1password.com). He liked that you can read what the product will and will not do. He did not want the corporate blue or the "start a trial" tone.

## Layout Plan

The sketch is not in the repo yet. I still need to draw it by hand or in Figma and commit the image. The description I used as the starting prompt is below.

Home. Black page. A header with a B mark, the word BLACKBOX, and the same four links on every page: Home, How it works, Why, and Get access. The hero is two columns. Left: a small eyebrow, the word BLACKBOX set very large with BOX in green, the line "Agent companies won't audit themselves.", two short sentences, and a green action that jumps to early access. Right: a receipt card, tilted a few degrees, titled THE RECORD IS YOURS, with three rows (a message recorded, a purchase reviewed, an approval that is your call) and a caption that the activity is illustrative. Under that, a three-column row: Receipts, Rules, The Gate. Then a wide quote that your rules should travel with you when you use more than one agent. Then a closing band that says email signup is not live and shows a control that does not accept an address. A thin footer with the year and "Your agents. Your rules."

How it works. Same header. A page title, "The layer between intent and action." Three bands. Copy on one side, an example panel on the other, and the middle band flips sides. The panels are a receipt, a short policy list (spend up to $50, ask before an investor message, ask before a public post), and a gate that asks before a message goes to a new contact. Each panel says it is an example and that the choices do nothing. The close links back to the early-access status on the home page.

Why. Same header. Title: "Trust is not a setting." One column of short paragraphs. Green emphasis on the words act, Receipts, Rules, and The Gate. Same early-access close as How it works.

On a phone, the header stacks, the hero and the three columns become one column, and the receipt sits flat instead of tilted. The links stay visible. No menu that hides them.
